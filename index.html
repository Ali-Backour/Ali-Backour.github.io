Preprint, 2026

Guidance as Variance Matching

Ali Backour*, Artem Lukoianov*, Frank Permenter, Tommi Jaakkola, Justin Solomon, Vincent Sitzmann (* equal contribution)

Guidance substantially improves the sample quality of diffusion models, but its mechanism remains poorly understood. We find that, at the scales used in practice, guidance makes the prediction of the posterior mean worse, while samples improve. Posterior-mean predictions have less variance than the data, and guidance restores it. We introduce variance-matched guidance: a closed-form rule that picks the guidance scale by matching the variance of the guided predictions to that of the data. Without tuning, it recovers the hand-picked scales of eleven widely used models across image, video, audio, motion, and world generation. Our interpretation also suggests that guidance can work with negative scales, which our experiments support.

  • Guidance

NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Interpretability as a Science

The Dynamics of Continuous Mixture Collapse in Language Models

Ali Backour

Latent-state reasoning methods replace discrete intermediate tokens with continuous states, such as mixtures of token embeddings, but pretrained language models often fail to preserve these mixtures. I identify three distinct sources of failure. Transformer architectures distort mixture geometry, and training amplifies this. Even with perfectly linear transport, the softmax readout and autoregressive feedback form a dynamical system that either amplifies small differences until one component dominates or contracts mixtures until they become indistinguishable; the observed transition occurs near the predicted threshold, and pretrained-model rollouts lie mostly on the amplifying side. Finally, exact preservation of many-component mixtures generally requires a context-dependent correction whose dimensionality can grow with the number of components.

  • Latent reasoning

NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Beyond Private Training: The New Landscape of AI Privacy

Encrypt What Matters: When Selective Homomorphic Inference Is Efficient

Ali Backour, Juan Reyes, Jaime Punyed, Ana Onoprishvili

Studied selective homomorphic inference, where only a sensitive region of interest is encrypted under FHE and computations independent of it run in plaintext. This produces the same output as full FHE on the same model, without retraining, and its efficiency depends on how quickly encrypted dependencies spread through the network. For small encrypted regions, locality-preserving architectures achieve order-of-magnitude speedups, while architectures with early global mixing gain essentially nothing.

  • Privacy-preserving ML

NeurIPS 2025 Workshop on Machine Learning and the Physical Sciences

Implicit Augmentation from Distributional Symmetry in Turbulence Super-Resolution

Julia Balla, Jeremiah Bailey, Ali Backour, Elyssa Hofgard, Tommi Jaakkola, Tess Smidt, Ryley McConkey

Studied whether convolutional neural networks can acquire rotational equivariance implicitly from turbulence data, without explicit rotation augmentation or specialized equivariant architectures. We found that statistical isotropy in turbulent flows acts as a natural source of augmentation: models trained on more isotropic mid-plane data exhibit lower equivariance error than those trained near anisotropic boundary layers, and increasing temporal or spatial sampling further improves equivariance.

  • Turbulence

6.S094: Deep Learning Alchemy

Instructor, with Danial Hosseintabar · Sponsored by Phillip Isola

Deep learning is full of surprising empirical phenomena that remain poorly understood. This class goes through them alongside the theories that try to explain them: scaling laws, training dynamics and optimization, architecture, and generalization, with an emphasis on language models. Students run the experiments themselves on a transformer, and are then tested the way research tests you: by predicting the outcome of an experiment they have not run. As coding agents make implementation cheap, asking good questions and designing informative experiments matter more than ever, and this class is built to train exactly that.
